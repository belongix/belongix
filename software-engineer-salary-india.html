Software Engineer Salary by Experience (India, 2026)

Experience is the single biggest driver of software engineer compensation in India. The jump from fresher to 3 years is significant — typically 2–3x salary — because that's when engineers become productive independently. After 5–6 years, specialisation (AI/ML, cloud, platform) becomes the primary salary lever.

How to Negotiate a Higher Software Engineer Salary in India

Most software engineers in India accept the first offer they receive — and leave significant money on the table. Based on Belongix community data, engineers who negotiate effectively earn ₹2–6 LPA more on average. Here's a 4-step framework:

1
Research before the first call, not after the offer

Use Belongix Salary Intelligence or AmbitionBox to understand the full compensation range for your target role at that specific company type. Know P25, median, and P75 for your experience band — you'll use the P75 number as your anchor in negotiation.

2
Never give a number first — and deflect the current salary question

When asked "What is your current CTC?", respond: "I'm focused on the market rate for this role, which I understand is ₹[X–Y range]. Can you share the budgeted range for this position?" This anchors the conversation to market, not your starting point.

3
Counter 15–20% above the first offer

The first offer is almost never the best offer. Counter with your researched P75 number and justify it with 2–3 specific achievements (quantified impact is much stronger than job responsibilities). Companies typically have 10–20% wiggle room on base, and often more flexibility on ESOPs or joining bonus.

4
Always negotiate total compensation, not just base

If they can't move on base, push on: joining bonus (1–3 months salary is common at startups), ESOPs / RSUs (often more valuable than base at high-growth companies), work-from-home allowance (₹5,000–15,000/month at many companies), and learning budget (₹30,000–1,00,000/year at top companies).

Frequently Asked Questions — Software Engineer Salary India 2026

What is the average software engineer salary in India in 2026?+
The average software engineer salary in India in 2026 is approximately ₹16 LPA (median) across all experience levels and company types. Entry-level engineers at product companies earn ₹8–14 LPA, mid-level (3–5 years) earn ₹16–30 LPA, and senior engineers (6+ years) earn ₹28–60 LPA. FAANG and top unicorns pay significantly higher — up to ₹130 LPA for principal/staff roles.
Which city pays software engineers the most in India?+
Bangalore pays the highest software engineer salaries in India, with a median of ₹19 LPA — 19% above the national average. Remote roles with Bangalore-headquartered companies now often pay the same or more (₹22L+ median). Hyderabad is second at ₹17 LPA. Mumbai, while expensive to live in, pays engineers slightly below Bangalore due to having fewer pure product-tech companies.
How much do FAANG companies pay software engineers in India?+
FAANG companies (Google, Amazon, Meta, Apple, Microsoft) pay SDE-1 engineers ₹25–40 LPA, SDE-2 ₹40–65 LPA, and senior/staff engineers ₹70–130 LPA. Total compensation including RSUs can reach ₹1.5–2Cr+ for senior L5/L6 engineers. Note that FAANG offers include a mix of base salary (typically lower), annual bonus (10–20%), and stock grants (often the largest component).
What is a good salary for a software engineer with 3 years experience?+
A software engineer with 3 years of experience should expect ₹18–28 LPA at a top startup or unicorn in Bangalore, ₹14–22 LPA at a mid-tier product company, or ₹8–14 LPA at an IT services company. If you're earning below ₹14 LPA with 3 years at a product company in Bangalore or Hyderabad, you are likely underpaid by 20–40% relative to market.
Does switching jobs increase salary faster than staying?+
Yes — job switching in India consistently results in 30–50% salary increases versus 8–15% typical annual raises. Belongix community data shows engineers who switch every 2–3 years earn 60–80% more over a 10-year period than those who stay at one company. The most effective switches happen when you have a competing offer to use as leverage — even if you don't intend to take it.
