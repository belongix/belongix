Why most Indians don't negotiate (and lose ₹lakhs)
5 min

Research shows that 72% of Indian professionals accept the first offer they receive without negotiating. The average money left on the table: ₹2.8 LPA per job change. Over a career, that compounds to ₹1 crore+.

Why we don't negotiate

  • Fear of offer being rescinded (this almost never happens — <0.1%)
  • Don't know market rate (fixable in 20 minutes)
  • "They said the budget is fixed" (always a negotiating tactic)
  • Imposter syndrome — "I don't deserve more"

💡 Key insight: Companies expect you to negotiate. The first offer is almost never the best offer. Recruiters have a range — your job is to anchor closer to the top of it.

Research: Know your market rate in 20 minutes
8 min

Your 3 data sources

  1. Belongix Salary Intelligence — Dashboard → Salary Insights → filter your role + city. Shows P25, median, P75, and top 10%.
  2. AmbitionBox — search your target company + role. Note: self-reported data, skews 10-15% low, but useful for company-specific ranges.
  3. LinkedIn Salary — requires 5+ connections at the company but gives the most accurate data.

What number to use in negotiation

Use the P75 (75th percentile) for your target number. This is what top performers earn — you're not below average. Your counter-offer should be at or slightly above P75.

📊 Example: If the market range for "Backend Engineer, 4 yrs, Bangalore" is ₹18–32 LPA, the median is ~₹24L and P75 is ~₹28L. Counter at ₹28–30L.

The negotiation conversation — scripts that work
10 min

Rule 1: Never give a number first

When asked "What is your expected CTC?", respond:
"I'm focused on finding the right fit and compensation that reflects the market and my experience. Could you share the budgeted range for this role?"

Rule 2: Counter 15–20% above their first offer

When they give you an offer of ₹20 LPA and you want ₹24L:
"Thank you for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about this role. Based on my research into market rates for this position in Bangalore, and given my [X specific skill/achievement], I was expecting something closer to ₹24–26 LPA. Is there flexibility to get there?"

Rule 3: "The budget is fixed" rebuttal

"I understand, and I appreciate you being transparent. If the base is fixed, could we explore other components — joining bonus, ESOPs, or a performance review at 6 months? I want to make this work."

💡 Always negotiate total comp, not just base: Joining bonus (1–3 months salary), ESOPs, WFH allowance (₹5,000–15,000/month), learning budget (₹30,000–1L/year), flexible timelines.

Salary negotiation via email — the template
7 min

When to negotiate by email vs phone

Email negotiation is underrated and often more effective — you have time to think, it creates a paper trail, and the recruiter can take it to their manager more easily.

The email template

Subject: Re: Offer — [Your Name] — [Role]


Hi [Recruiter Name],


Thank you for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about joining [Company] and contributing to [specific team/product].


I've reviewed the offer carefully and researched the market rate for this role in [City]. Based on my [X years] of experience in [skill], and benchmarking against similar roles, I was expecting the total compensation to be closer to ₹[X] LPA.


Is there flexibility to adjust the base, or explore a joining bonus to bridge the gap? I'm committed to joining and want to make this work.


Looking forward to your response.
Best,
[Your Name]

Negotiating as a fresher — it works too
5 min

Many freshers assume they can't negotiate. This is wrong. Even entry-level negotiation works — especially at product companies and startups.

What freshers can negotiate

  • Joining bonus: ₹50,000–2,00,000 is common at startups. Ask: "Is there a joining bonus available?"
  • Start date: More time before joining = more time to negotiate competing offers
  • Role title: "Junior Engineer" vs "Software Engineer" affects future negotiations significantly
  • Review timeline: Ask for a 3-month vs 6-month review for a salary correction

💡 Best fresher negotiation script: "I'm very excited about this offer. I received another offer for ₹[X] — is there any flexibility to match or come closer to that? I'm strongly leaning toward [Company]."
